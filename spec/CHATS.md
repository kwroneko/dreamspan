# Chats: identity, finding, forks

**Status**: Design discussion in progress, 2026-10-16. Nothing is built. [D] items are the owner's words (also in DESIGN §2.1); [P] items are proposals from the discussion, not confirmed. [O] items are open.

## Constraints

- **[D]** There are no "group chats" and no card chats: a chat has any number of cards. A chat starts from one card and characters are added and removed seamlessly; adding several on the first turn is a special case. No multi-card picker or multi-step creation.
- **[D]** Chats imported from ST must be easy to recognize and find.
- **[D]** One click on a portrait gives the chat and the card's definitions. No flow takes more steps than ST's.
- **[D]** The design must hold for any user's data and habits.
- **[D] Rejected:** the recent-chats list as home (unrecognizable rows, forks flattened); the character grid with derived group tiles (ST's data model); titles generated by a model (needs a configured model, and same-card chats start alike).

## Opening a character vs opening a chat

- **[D]** These are separate ideas. A grid of characters you click opens a character, with the chats associated with it, not "a chat". A list of the last few chats, with an excerpt of their last message, sits alongside the grid, ordered by latest message by default.
- **[D]** The character view (owner's sketch): chat selector on the left, the chat in the middle, character data on the right.
- **[D]** The screen is three independent parts that some actions switch together (owner, 2026-10-26), refining the sketch above: the **left panel** usually shows a chat list; the **right panel**, when open, shows chat details and/or character details; the **middle** is the chat. There is no separate "character view" that every chat must belong to. Clicking a character switches the left panel to "chats with this character", ordered by when that character spoke, and opens that character in the right panel.
- **[D]** The left panel must show plainly what it is listing and how to get out of it, for example breadcrumbs for being "in a character" (owner, 2026-10-26).
- **[D]** Browsing the left panel never changes the chat in the middle: clicking the breadcrumb root or a higher level shows those chats, and the open chat stays until another chat is picked (owner, 2026-10-26).
- **[D]** A character's chats are ordered by when that character last spoke in them, most recent first. No other order is coherent: anything else lets unrelated activity bump a character's list (owner, 2026-10-26).
- **[D]** Clicking a character (in the grid or anywhere characters are listed) switches all three: the left panel to that character's chats, the right panel to that character, and the middle to the chat it spoke in last (owner, 2026-10-16 and 2026-10-26). Browsing within the left panel switches nothing else.
- **[P]** A character's chats are ordered by when that character last spoke in them, so a chat they left sinks as the story goes on without them. Opening a chat from a character lands on the line where that character spoke last. Chats they're no longer in on any line go in a "Was in" section.
- **[P]** The selector has two sections: "Just X" (X is the only character the model writes; personas and cards you play don't count) and "With others". The portrait opens the chat X spoke in last.
- **[P], superseded below** A chat is listed under the character, with a badge for the variant it uses; opening a variant filters to its chats.
- **[O]** That assumes a well-defined main variant (owner, 2026-10-16), and there isn't one. The data model has a root (the imported or upstream base) with variants as changes over it, possibly variants of variants; but the root is just where the lineage started, often a version the user no longer uses. And duplicates imported from ST are separate cards with no lineage at all.
- **[D] Rejected:** one tile per lineage showing its most recently used variant (owner, 2026-10-16: "I don't get what purpose this serves"). It adds a layer, and a UI to pick the variant, everywhere a character is chosen.
- **[P]** Each variant is a card of its own wherever cards appear, told apart by its display name and subtitle (DESIGN §4). The lineage is a relationship, like a named fork's: the card shows "variant of ⟨card⟩" and its other versions, without grouping them. A chat is listed under the exact card it uses, so no variant badge is needed. Making a variant asks for the display name and subtitle up front. The subtitle is the card's own `character_version` field (DESIGN §4).
- **[D]** Making a variant is **forking** the card; a fork is a child of its parent card. There must be an easy way to find all variants of a card (owner, 2026-10-16).
- **[P]** Fork semantics for cards, the same words as for chats: "Fork" replaces "Make a variant"; a fork shows "forked from ⟨parent⟩"; the card view has a **Forks** section with the whole family as a tree (from the card nobody forked down to every fork of a fork), each entry with display name, subtitle and when it was last used, one click to open. A tile for a card with forks shows how many it has, and that count opens the same tree. Linking an imported ST duplicate is "mark as a fork of ⟨card⟩". A chat customizes a card with Fork for this chat, an ordinary fork swapped in (CARD-FORKS.md; the earlier chat-scoped fork is withdrawn). Deleting a parent leaves its forks as they are.
- **[D] Rejected:** parent changes flowing into forks (owner, 2026-10-16; DESIGN §4). **[P]** A fork is a full copy from the moment it's made; only the relationship ties it to its parent.
- **[O]** Whether "last spoke" should count a character who is present but silent for a long stretch.

## Chats with several characters

- **[O]** How to show cast controls and settings. Owner: perhaps the same for every chat, or shown when there's more than one card, noting the persona is a card too.
- **[P]** One layout for every chat; the cast section is always there. Controls that only make sense with several characters (who replies next) appear when the model writes two or more.
- **[O]** How chats with several characters are found at the top level, now that groups don't exist ("being able to get to a groupchat from a character is not a bad thing in itself").
- **[P]** Pinned chats: a tile in the grid with the chat's faces and title, opening that chat. Each ST group imports as a pin to the chat that was current in ST, with the group's name and collage. Any chat can be pinned; nothing is grouped automatically.

## Forks

- **[D]** A named fork is shown at the top level like any other chat, while preserving the fork relationship (owner, 2026-10-16: "probably").
- **[P]** Unnamed forks stay inside their chat: in the selector the chat expands to its lines, each with its latest message; in the stream, a fork shows at the message where it splits.
- **[P]** A named fork appears wherever chats appear (recents, pins, character selectors) with its own faces, excerpt and title, and "forked from ⟨chat⟩ at message N". Its parent lists it by name among its lines.
- **[P]** Activity on a named fork bumps the fork, not its parent. Unnamed forks count toward their parent.
- **[P]** Removing the name returns it to its parent's lines; nothing moves in the data. A named fork of a named fork points at its immediate parent.
- **[P]** History before the fork point is shared: an edit or a new version there shows in both, and the editor says so ("also in ⟨fork⟩").
- **[P]** Forks the user named in ST arrive as named forks (built on branch `claude/compassionate-rhodes-787c7d`, d30483d, not merged). ST names a fork file `Branch #<message> - <date>`, or `Checkpoint #<n> - <date>` (`Bookmark #` in older versions) when the name is left empty; any other name is the user's, minus the ` - <date>` suffix, and so is a fork file renamed in ST's chat list. **Today the importer drops it:** a branch file merges into its parent chat and its file name is discarded (`stImportWrites.ts`, title applied only when not merged).
- **[O]** Which version is selected in shared history: per line or shared.
- **[O]** How long a chat's list of lines gets before the selector needs more than expanding it.
