# UX rework: where the design stands


## 1. Where things stand

- **Code:** master = `origin/master` (`dreamspan`). Nothing from this discussion is built: it was design only, and no code changes until the design is agreed. One exception, on a branch: `refs/heads/claude/compassionate-rhodes-787c7d` (d30483d) imports the names users gave forks in ST as branch names, and re-import fills in missing names without overwriting app-set ones. It's not reviewed or merged; three scale tests timed out in its full run under load and pass alone.

## 2. The owner's position and how to work

- **D** The app won't be used until it is clearly better than ST: once the owner starts, wiping the data and re-importing stops being possible. So nothing waits on the owner "trying it"; walkthroughs on realistic synthetic data stand in.
- **D** The objective was never to clone ST ("ST fucking sucks"). The build adopted many ST concepts against the design. ST is a floor for what you can reach and how fast, never a source of concepts, structure or labels.
- **D** Designs must hold for any user's data and habits, not the owner's own.
- **D** Most users' chats, the owner's included, come from ST, so imported chats must be easy to recognize and find.
- Process lessons: design whole flows across every surface they touch, from the users' tasks; don't patch findings one by one; don't ask the owner to approve a plan, ask only real decisions in chat with their trade-offs.

## 3. Decided

### Chats and finding them
- **D** No "group chats" and no card chats: a chat has any number of cards. A chat starts from one card; characters are added and removed seamlessly at any point. Several on the first turn is a special case: no multi-card picker, no multi-step creation.
- **D** One click on a portrait gives both the chat and the card's definitions, as in ST. No flow may take more steps than ST's. The current two buttons on each portrait (chat / card) are a regression.
- **D** "Opening a character" and "opening a chat" are separate ideas. A grid of characters opens a character, with the chats associated with it (it can open on the latest chat the character is in, but that is still opening the character). Beside the grid, a list of the last few chats with an excerpt of their last message, ordered by latest message.
- **D** The character view (owner's sketch): chat selector on the left, the chat in the middle, character data on the right.
- **D** The screen is three independent parts that some actions switch together (owner, 2026-10-26), refining the sketch above: the **left panel** usually shows a chat list; the **right panel**, when open, shows chat details and/or character details; the **middle** is the chat. There is no separate "character view" that every chat must belong to. Clicking a character switches the left panel to "chats with this character", ordered by when that character spoke, and opens that character in the right panel.
- **D** The left panel must show plainly what it is listing and how to get out of it, for example breadcrumbs for being "in a character" (owner, 2026-10-26).
- **D** Browsing the left panel never changes the chat in the middle: clicking the breadcrumb root or a higher level shows those chats, and the open chat stays until another chat is picked (owner, 2026-10-26).
- **D** A character's chats are ordered by when that character last spoke in them, most recent first. No other order is coherent: anything else lets unrelated activity bump a character's list (owner, 2026-10-26).
- **D** Clicking a character (in the grid or anywhere characters are listed) switches all three: the left panel to that character's chats, the right panel to that character, and the middle to the chat it spoke in last (owner, 2026-10-16 and 2026-10-26). Browsing within the left panel switches nothing else.
- **D** For reference, ST: the main screen shows the three most recent chats; the character and group grid is always in a side panel, which picks and searches characters and groups; clicking one replaces the grid with the card's definitions and the main view becomes its (only) current chat.
- **D Rejected:** the recent-chats list as home (the first UI: rows like "Jane Doe - 2026-04-23@6:00 AM", forks flattened among them); the character grid with derived group tiles (ST's data model, though it does make imports findable); titles generated by a model (needs a configured model, and chats with the same card start alike, so it's a costlier way of naming them after the card).

- **D** The character grid keeps a **Groups** filter even without a group-chat concept: it shows chats with more than one character (plus the user) in the same kind of tile. That switches the same place from showing cards to showing chats under a filter; acceptable for now (owner, 2026-10-26). **O** Whether that turns out too inconsistent.
- **D** **Never used** is a good distinction and stays (owner, 2026-10-26).

### Home page and the left panel (owner, 2026-10-26, from the layout mockups)
- **D** `/` is the home page with the character grid, as now. Once in a chat you never *need* to go back to it, but it's what `/` shows. Whether home has a grid and whether the side panel has one are separate questions: both do.
- **D** The left panel shows a chat list with breadcrumbs inside a more specific context (a character, a chat), and can also switch to a character thumbnail grid like the home page's, to jump between characters quickly.
- **D** Home's tabs: **All | With chats | Never used | Groups | Archive** (the archive tab's name is to be decided). Sort: **Recent | Created | Name**. *Recent* is the card's most recent change: added, modified, or it emitted a turn. *Created* is when the card was added and never changes; imports must take it from ST (`create_date`, else the file's creation time, as ST does).
- **D** **Groups** shows, instead of characters, a grid of chats with more than one character (plus the user): a top-level way into group chats without going through a character, as ST mixed group chats in with characters. It isn't mixed into the other tabs, for now.
- **D** A chat's **current fork** is the one last chatted in or created by forking, whichever is more recent. Forks promoted to peers (named, pinned) have equal status. The Groups grid shows each chat's current fork and its peers, and a click opens the current fork; the chat's other forks are then selectable in the left panel, which scopes to that chat. (This corrects the earlier note that there is no current fork.)
- **D** **Pinned chats** sit above the tabs at the top of the home page, only when there are any. They're also at the top of the left panel, scoped to its level: with a character selected, only pins with that character; inside a chat, only that chat's pinned forks. Pinned chats have their own Recent | Created | Name sort and nothing else. The pinned group can be collapsed, remembered per account.
- **D Rejected** (the mockups' home): "My cards | Archive" above "Pinned chats", which isn't coherent; and dropping **Never used**, which is extremely useful.

### The chat view (owner, 2026-10-26, from the layout mockups)
- **D** Portraits in the chat are ST's size, with a setting to make them bigger or smaller.
- **D** Clicking a portrait in the chat shows the full image in a large panel beside it, as ST does. Fundamental.
- **D** The composer has at least everything today's composer has.
- **D Rejected** in the right panel: a "lines and forks" section, and a full section for the chat's origin (not something you need to keep looking at).

### Portraits
- **D** Portraits are rectangular, the shape of a card, everywhere: character tiles, chat rows, messages, cast chips, panels. ST uses rectangular avatars and thumbnails; the app mixes round and square (chats have round avatars), which makes no sense. One shape, used consistently (owner, 2026-10-26).
- **P** The card's own aspect ratio, 2:3 (ST's thumbnails and card images), at every size.

### Forks of chats
- **D** For the user, forks are separate chats that don't influence each other (owner, 2026-10-26). They are connected by history and in the data model, and as siblings when browsing chats (and can be named and promoted), but nothing done in one silently changes another: a fork is an explicit deviation, and any other model is incredibly confusing.
- **D** ("probably") A named fork is shown at the top level like any other chat, keeping its fork relationship.
- **D** Forks can already be named in ST; those names must be used (built on the branch above).

### Cards: names, forking, customizing for a chat
- **D** Three names: the **in-character name** (`{{char}}`, sent to the model); a **display name** (the in-character name by default, can differ, then it's what the UI shows); a **subtitle**, which is the card's own version field, ST's `character_version`, a real card field with its revisions that travels with the card file.
- **D** No two cards may share both display name and version. Enforced in the UI when making forks or new cards; not a data-model rule, since imports won't respect it.
- **D** Making a variant is **forking** the card: parent and child. There must be an easy way to find every variant of a card.
- **D Rejected:** forks that follow their parent (parent changes merged into them). It can overwrite the user's changes, nothing keeps the result coherent, it's hidden and surprising, and it doesn't replace duplicating. The build does this today.
- **D** Customizing a card for one chat is the typical case: **fork and swap** ("Fork for this chat"), which makes a concrete card that the chat uses instead. Forking the chat from anywhere earlier keeps the changed card; existing forks of the chat keep what they had. A fork made this way is an ordinary fork, with its tile, reachable to switch back to.
- **D** Card changes that follow the messages (made at a point, reset by branching before it) are a specific mechanism, not the default, and unintuitive to users; they also invalidate the cache by default. Their main purpose is the agent rewriting the card during a turn, which must always work this way so turns and swipes stay reversible. Manually, they serve character progression.
- **D** Character progression needs a more convenient, typical path: adding and changing things connected to the card and tied to the card *and* the chat (author's notes for characters), not editing the card.

### Editing and saving
- **D** Text boxes work like a code editor, and almost every text box needs it: new / modified / unchanged lines marked at the left edge; a diff of current changes against the last revision; a diff browser for every earlier revision. (CodeMirror 6's merge package covers the markers and both diffs.)
- **D** Continuous saving rules out real revisions and diff views, so saving stays explicit.

### State that follows the messages
- **D** Branching from an earlier point resets everything that follows the messages, which is less intuitive than it seems: many changes are wanted even in a fork that goes back earlier.
- **D** For notes it depends on the note (background and story-level notes aren't tied to the message they were last edited at). Harder than for cards: there are many notes and they don't modify something that already existed. A per-note setting is possible but must not become confusing.

### Snippets
- **D** A text snippet system is wanted, separate from notes, needing major UI design. Snippets are reachable from and to anywhere: copy part of any field into a snippet edited beside it, and bring snippet text into any field. Replaces the owner's unsaved notepad tabs. Example: part of a message or swipe from another branch into this one.

## 4. Proposed, not confirmed

**Chats and finding**
- A character's chats ordered by when that character last spoke in them; opening from a character lands on the line where they last spoke; chats they're in on no line go in a "Was in" section.
- The character view's selector split into "Just X" (the model writes only X; personas and cards you play don't count) and "With others".
- Chat identity without a model: cast faces, latest-message excerpt, message count and last activity, shown together everywhere a chat appears. A title is optional and the user's own; imported ST group names are kept as titles (user-written); ST file names are never shown; an untitled chat shows its cast's names, derived, not stored, so renaming or recasting never moves a chat anywhere.
- Pinned chats: a tile in the grid with the chat's faces and title. Each ST group imports as a pin to the chat that was current in ST.
- One layout for every chat; cast section always there; multi-character controls (who replies next) appear when the model writes two or more characters.

**Forks of chats**
- Unnamed forks stay inside their chat: the selector expands a chat into its lines with their latest messages; the stream shows a fork where it splits.
- A named fork: own entry everywhere chats appear, "forked from ⟨chat⟩ at message N"; its parent lists it by name; its activity bumps it, not the parent; un-naming returns it to the parent; shared history before the fork point shows edits in both, and the editor says so.

**Cards** ([CARD-FORKS.md](CARD-FORKS.md) has every surface)
- Fork dialog asks for the three names up front; the new card opens in the editor; parent and chats untouched.
- Forks section in the card panel: the whole family as a tree, the same from any member, with names, last used and chat count. Fork count on a tile opens it.
- "Use another version" per cast member swaps the card the branch uses.
- Compare with parent or any family member (read-only diff). "Mark as a fork of…" / Unlink for ST duplicates. Re-importing a card file updates only that card. Deleting a card leaves its forks. Personas fork the same way.
- What you edit is chosen before typing: the chat's card panel names the card the branch uses, with "used by N chats", and offers Fork for this chat.
- A draft of a card shows wherever that card is opened, marked unsaved with Save (diff) and Discard, and never reaches the model; the panel says so.
- The display-name/version check also applies when renaming an existing card.
- Message-following card changes take a whole copy of the card at their first change, so nothing merges into them later (this also replaces DESIGN §4.1a's field patches over a moving card).

**Notes and snippets**
- A note belongs to the branch by default; each generation records which note revisions it used; forking from an earlier point lists the notes changed since, each with "as it was then" / "as it is now" (default now). Agent-made note changes always follow the messages.
- Snippets kept on the server like drafts (live across tabs), scoped global or one chat including its forks; the composition scratchpad becomes one use of snippets; notes stay simple and can be filled from a snippet.
